Election promises

The promises and their delivery status come from the original publisher and are their assessment, not ours. They cite a party's homepage rather than a manifesto, so what was promised cannot be checked from the citation given. Audit India has verified neither the wording nor the status.

Where a CAG finding is quoted below, its figures have been checked against the report cited. Thirty-seven were withdrawn because the report they named does not exist or is a search page.

Women & Social Welfare

  • Give every household three free gas cylinders a year, and ₹1,500 a month to every woman aged 19 to 59 — two of the six "Super Six" pledges.

    Partly delivered

    Deepam 2.0 launched July 2024: free gas cylinder per month to Antyodaya/BPL/AAY households (starting at 1 cylinder/month); ₹1,500 cash to "eligible" women. Implementation has been selective — full ₹1,500/month to all women not achieved; cylinder delivery behind schedule in some districts due to supply chain constraints. Budget pressure cited.

  • Free bus travel for women on all APSRTC buses

    Delivered

    Free bus pass for women on APSRTC launched shortly after TDP-NDA government took office (June 2024). Scheme covers ordinary and express APSRTC services; ~1.5 crore women beneficiaries. APSRTC loss of ~₹1,500 crore/year compensated by GoAP subsidy.

Agriculture & Farmers

  • Anna Canteen: meals at ₹5 and free food for poor

    Delivered

    Anna Canteen reopened by TDP government (Jagan had closed it in 2019). 196 canteens operational across 13 districts serving ~3 lakh meals/day at ₹5. Free meals for below-poverty beneficiaries. TDP fulfilled this promise quickly as it was part of the "Super 6" headline.

  • Rytu Bharosa: ₹20,000/year input assistance to all farmers

    Partly delivered

    Jagan's YSR Rythu Bharosa was ₹13,500/year (₹7,500 state + PM-KISAN ₹6,000). TDP promised to enhance to ₹20,000/year. In 2024-25, GoAP paid ₹9,000 (state) + PM-KISAN ₹6,000 = ₹15,000 — improvement but below the ₹20,000 promise. Further enhancement subject to fiscal space.

Infrastructure & Capital

  • Resume and complete Amaravati capital city construction by 2027

    Partly delivered

    TDP top priority after taking office: Amaravati construction resumed June 2024 after 5-year Jagan-era freeze. Singapore-designed capital plan restarted; contractors remobilised. Key buildings (High Court, Secretariat) at 50-80% completion when frozen; resumption is proceeding but 2027 completion for full city is highly ambitious — most estimates suggest 2029+.

Electricity

  • Free electricity up to 200 units per month for all households

    Partly delivered

    TDP delivered free power to agriculture (7-9 hours/day) and BPL households (up to 100 units free) from 2024. The 200-unit free scheme for all households not fully operationalised; middle-income households benefit from lower slab rates but not zero-bill. DISCOMs' accumulated losses (~₹90,000 crore from YSRCP era) constrain full delivery.

Irrigation

  • Complete Polavaram Irrigation Project — the lifeline of Andhra Pradesh

    Partly delivered

    CAG Performance Audit of the Polavaram Irrigation Project (2025): the cost estimate rose from ₹16,010.45 crore (2010-11 price level, first revised estimate) to ₹55,548.87 crore (2017-18 price level, accepted February 2019) — a 247 per cent increase. The project involves displacement of 1.06 lakh project-displaced families, whose rehabilitation the audit found had made meagre progress, both physically and financially.

Employment & Industry

  • Swarna Andhra 2047: create 20 lakh jobs; attract ₹10 lakh crore investment in 5 years

    Partly delivered

    TDP hosted Andhra Pradesh Global Investors Summit (APGIS) in March 2025, attracting MOUs worth ₹12.5 lakh crore — though actual FDI realisation is typically 10–15% of MOU value. Semiconductor/electronics investment by FoxConn, TATA Electronics and others announced for Kopparthy electronics manufacturing cluster. 20 lakh job target has no verified tracking mechanism. AP's chronic fiscal crisis (revenue deficit ~₹30,000 crore/year, inherited from YSRCP term) constrains public sector employment.

Sand Mining & Minerals

  • End illegal sand mining mafia; supply free sand to AP homebuilders

    Partly delivered

    TDP promised to dismantle the sand syndicate that flourished under YSRCP and resume free sand supply to house-construction beneficiaries. Free sand scheme reinstated for PMAY-G and housing beneficiaries in select districts. However river-bed illegal mining continues in Krishna and Godavari. The ₹11,000+ crore sand mining revenue target for 2024-25 was not met.

    CAG Report No. 1 of 2025 (Performance Audit on the Functioning of the Mines and Geology Department): Andhra Pradesh had still not framed a State Mineral Policy. Mining plans were approved without applying the United Nations Framework Classification, inflating both mineral reserves and the life of leases. The department allowed lessees to excavate without environmental clearance and beyond the permitted quantity, contrary to Supreme Court directions, and did not recover the cost of what was illegally extracted. Mineral revenue assessments and demand-collection-balance registers were not maintained, and minerals taken for government works needed no clearance certificate at all, so the department did not know what had been extracted or what was owed.

Healthcare

  • Restructure and strengthen AP's public health infrastructure; 24×7 PHCs statewide

    Partly delivered

    AP has 10,032 sub-centres and 1,650 PHCs; under TDP, upgrading 350 PHCs to 24×7 is in progress. YSR Aarogyasri (₹5 lakh health cover) continued from YSRCP. The CAG performance audit of public health infrastructure (Report No. 3 of 2024) found 743 of 3,316 sanctioned specialist and medical officer posts vacant in secondary healthcare, and intensive care units missing altogether in five of 12 district hospitals.

    CAG Report No. 3 of 2024 (Performance Audit on Public Health Infrastructure and Management of Health Services in Andhra Pradesh): 743 of 3,316 sanctioned specialist and medical officer posts were vacant in secondary healthcare, and 149 of 608 Professor and 156 of 744 Associate Professor posts were vacant across 11 government medical colleges. Intensive care units were absent in five of 12 district hospitals, and equipment in intensive care and obstetric high-dependency units lay non-functional for want of staff. No test-checked secondary facility had the full range of laboratory services. Near-expiry drugs worth ₹2.14 crore were distributed to health facilities from three central drug stores.

Social Welfare & Pensions

  • YSR Pension Kanuka: ₹2,500-10,000/month for elderly, disabled and widows

    Delivered

    YSR Pension Kanuka launched 2019: ₹2,500/month to senior citizens, widows, disabled, weavers and toddy tappers. Enhanced to ₹2,750 (2021) and ₹3,000 (2023). 60 lakh beneficiaries; delivered door-to-door by volunteers each month on 11th of the month. Considered YSRCP's most successful and visible delivery.

Agriculture

  • YSR Rythu Bharosa: ₹13,500/year input assistance to all AP farmers

    Delivered

    YSR Rythu Bharosa: ₹7,500 state + ₹6,000 PM-KISAN = ₹13,500 annually. 56 lakh farmer families enrolled. Paid in 3 instalments. Consistently delivered through the 2019-24 YSRCP term; one of the larger state-level farmer support programmes in India.

  • YSR Zero Interest Loan Scheme for SHG women (0% interest on loans up to ₹3 lakh)

    Delivered

    YSR Sunna Vaddi: government pays interest on SHG loans up to ₹3 lakh; 27 lakh SHG women benefited across DWACRA groups. Annual interest subvention ~₹800 crore. Recognised as a major driver of rural women's economic participation in AP.

Education & Health

  • Jagananna Amma Vodi: ₹15,000/year for mothers whose children attend government school

    Delivered

    Amma Vodi launched January 2020: ₹15,000/year paid directly to eligible mothers who enrol their children in government schools. Enrolled 43 lakh children; 37 lakh mothers benefited annually. Government school enrolment increased significantly — from 38 lakh (2019) to 54 lakh (2023). One of India's most innovative school attendance incentive schemes.

  • YSR Aarogyasri: enhance healthcare coverage to ₹5 lakh per family annually

    Delivered

    YSR Aarogyasri enhanced from ₹2.5 lakh (TDP era) to ₹5 lakh/year per family (2019). 72 lakh families covered at 1,000+ empanelled hospitals. Treatment packages expanded to 2,826 procedures. Dialysis, cardiac procedures and cancer treatment added. Scheme considered YSRCP's key healthcare achievement.

Capital & Governance

  • Three capitals model: Legislative (Amaravati), Executive (Vizag), Judicial (Kurnool)

    Not delivered

    YSRCP attempted a three-capital model to decentralise governance and balance regional development. Legislature passed the AP Decentralisation Act 2020; AP High Court issued a stay. Three-capital plan effectively stalled for 4 of the 5-year term; no executive functions shifted to Vizag. AP HC stay and legal battles consumed much of the tenure. TDP reversed the plan entirely in 2024.

  • Village and Ward Secretariat system: government services at the doorstep in every village

    Delivered

    YSRCP's most ambitious governance reform: 10,778 village secretariats and 3,732 ward secretariats established with multi-function civil servants serving 50–100 households each. Services include pensions, ration cards, health referrals and certificates. Considered a landmark in last-mile delivery. TDP retained the secretariat system (renaming it) after 2024; the basic architecture survived the change of government — a testament to its effectiveness.

Irrigation

  • Complete Polavaram Project and provide irrigation to 7.5 lakh acres in AP delta

    Not delivered

    YSRCP inherited Polavaram from TDP (which had started construction post-bifurcation). Under Jagan (2019–2024) the project's cost estimate was revised to ₹55,548.87 crore at 2017-18 prices — against ₹16,010.45 crore at 2010-11 prices, a 247 per cent increase — making it the most expensive state irrigation project in India. Coffer dam breached in 2020, 2021 and 2022 floods, each time causing delays and losses. Tribal R&R resettlement remained incomplete for over 1.06 lakh families. Project remains unfinished when YSRCP left power.

    CAG Report No. 4 of 2025 (Performance Audit on the Polavaram Irrigation Project): the headworks remained incomplete 17 years after work began; ₹7,422.69 crore of headworks was awarded and ₹5,757.48 crore spent. Because the spillway and spill channel were unfinished, the river could not be diverted and floodwater passed through gaps in the cofferdam, damaging both the cofferdams and the diaphragm wall — still not rectified as of March 2023. Of 1,06,006 displaced families, 56,495 of them tribal, only 11,677 — 11 per cent — had been rehabilitated.

Sand Mining & Natural Resources

  • End sand mafia; supply free sand to house-construction beneficiaries under government schemes

    Not delivered

    Jagan's 2019 promise of crushing the sand cartel and providing free sand was one of his most prominent pre-election pledges. Free sand scheme launched — but implementation became mired in YSRCP-linked contractors allegedly controlling district sand reaches. The sand mafia became one of the loudest TDP opposition attack lines during 2019-24, pointing to YSRCP insider involvement.

    CAG Report No. 1 of 2025 (Performance Audit on the Functioning of the Mines and Geology Department): the department did not comply with the Supreme Court's direction that minerals excavated without, or in excess of, an environmental clearance are illegal and their cost recoverable — it allowed such excavation and did not recover the cost. Mineral concession applications were processed opaquely, the committee constituted to monitor them was non-functional, inoperative leases were not resumed, and lease deeds went unexecuted, costing stamp duty and registration revenue.

Women Welfare

  • YSR Cheyutha: ₹75,000 over 4 years for BC/SC/ST/minority women entrepreneurs aged 45-60

    Delivered

    YSR Cheyutha launched 2020: ₹18,750/year × 4 years = ₹75,000 per beneficiary for women in the 45-60 age bracket from BC, SC, ST and minority communities to support self-employment. 23.64 lakh women enrolled; total outlay ~₹4,500 crore over the 4-year YSRCP term. Delivered without Aadhaar-seeding issues that plagued similar schemes in other states. Considered one of YSRCP's more targeted welfare successes.

  • End child marriage and improve adolescent girl nutrition in tribal areas

    Partly delivered

    AP child marriage rate declined from 32.9% (NFHS-4) to 24.8% (NFHS-5) during 2015-21 — a significant drop but still well above the national target. Anganwadi nutrition programmes in Alluri Sitharama Raju and Parvathipuram-Manyam improved reach. The CAG performance audit of public health infrastructure (Report No. 3 of 2024) records no anaemia prevalence figure for these districts; what it does record is that the department kept no centralised database of sanctioned posts, staff in position or unit-wise deployment anywhere in the health system.

    CAG Report No. 5 of 2025 (Compliance Audit on the Sampoorna Poshana Scheme), which audits the supplementary nutrition given to pregnant women, nursing mothers and children aged six to 72 months through Anganwadi centres, is the audit that covers this ground. The public health audit of 2024 carries no anaemia or Anganwadi vacancy figure for the tribal districts.
